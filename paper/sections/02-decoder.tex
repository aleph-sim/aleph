\section{The decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}

\subsection*{Code and error models}

The target is the gross code, the \gross{} bivariate-bicycle (BB) code of
Bravyi et al.~\citep{bravyi2024gross}: a CSS code over the group algebra of
$\mathbb{Z}_\ell\times\mathbb{Z}_m$ with $H_X=[A\,|\,B]$,
$H_Z=[B^{\mathsf T}\,|\,A^{\mathsf T}]$ for two commuting three-term
polynomials in the cyclic shifts $x,y$.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q5-gross.md "What and why" (ℓ, m, A, B) and "Results" table
The gross code is $\ell=12$, $m=6$, $A=x^3+y+y^2$, $B=y^3+x+x^2$: $n=144$,
$k=12$, $d=12$ (distance cited from~\citep{bravyi2024gross}; $n$, $k$
recomputed from GF(2) ranks), 72 $X$-checks and 72 $Z$-checks, every check of
weight~6 and every qubit of degree~3. Each error lights three checks, so the
syndrome graph is a hypergraph: matching decoders do not apply and belief
propagation (BP) is required.

Two detector error models (DEMs) feed the same decoder through a Tanner graph.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q5-gross.md "Results" (72 / 12 / 144, 432 edges)
The \emph{code-capacity} DEM (one perfect round, independent $Z$ error per
qubit) has 72 detectors, 12 observables, 144 mechanisms and $144\times3=432$
Tanner edges; its regular graph is what the unrolled RTL of
\S\ref{sec:architecture} bakes in, and it is the model of the fixed-point and
schedule studies below.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q5-circuit-dem.md "The depth-7 syndrome circuit" and "The noise model"
The \emph{circuit-level} DEM lays down the depth-7 syndrome-extraction
schedule of Bravyi et al.\ in a memory-$X$ experiment, with the $Z$-checks
measured at round~6 before that round's $X$-CNOTs so that no hook error
corrupts a stabiliser, and projects the circuit-level depolarising model (CNOT,
idle, preparation and measurement faults at rate $p$) to the $Z$ sector.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q5-circuit-dem.md "Correctness — Stim oracle"
It is verified edge-for-edge against Stim~\citep{gidney2021stim}: on the
$[[72,12,6]]$ member of the family at rounds $\in\{1,2,3\}$ with non-uniform
rates every edge probability agrees to below $10^{-9}$, and a clean Stim build
certifies the schedule is non-disturbing.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q5-circuit-dem.md "DEM structure" table
At $\mathrm{rounds}=d=12$ the gross-code circuit DEM has 936 detectors, 12
observables and 8784 mechanisms, about $13\times$ the detectors and $60\times$
the mechanisms of the code-capacity model.

\subsection*{Relay-BP min-sum}

% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M0" What and why (relay-BP vs BP+OSD rationale)
BP alone is degeneracy-limited on qLDPC codes: it oscillates or converges to a
valid but wrong-coset solution. The usual remedy, BP followed by
ordered-statistics decoding (BP-OSD)~\citep{panteleev2021bposd}, adds a
data-dependent GF(2) Gauss--Jordan elimination of unbounded latency. Relay-BP
\citep{muller2025relaybp} stays inside message passing: several \emph{legs} of
normalised min-sum BP, each reseeding a per-variable disordered memory
coefficient $\gamma_v$ and relaying the previous leg's messages, keeping the
lowest-weight syndrome-consistent solution seen. In our software comparison it
beat BP-OSD at every $p$ while dropping the elimination entirely, so it is the
hardware decoder.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q5-bposd.md "What and why" (α=1 fails 29 %, α=0.875 fails 2.3 % at p=0.04)
The check update is normalised min-sum with $\alpha=0.875$; plain $\alpha=1$
over-converged to wrong cosets on 29\,\% of shots at $p=0.04$ (code capacity),
against 2.3\,\% at $\alpha=0.875$. The (legs, iterations per leg) schedule is
fixed, so the worst-case latency is a constant.

\subsection*{Fixed-point word}

The bit-accurate golden model \texttt{FixedRelayBp} quantises the arithmetic
and is, by construction, the RTL specification.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M0" Fixed-point scheme
A value is stored as signed $\mathrm{round}(x\cdot2^F)$ saturated to
$\pm(2^{W-1}-1)$, with $W$ message bits and $F$ fractional bits. Since
$\alpha=7/8$, normalisation is $\mathrm{mag}-(\mathrm{mag}\gg3)$ and the whole
check update is compare/min/sign with no multiplier. The only multiply is the
relay blend $(1-\gamma_v)\cdot\mathrm{computed}+\gamma_v\cdot
m_{\mathrm{old}}$ with $\gamma_v$ a per-variable ROM constant, i.e.\ a
fixed-coefficient multiply. The blend truncates by arithmetic shift (floor),
the cheapest RTL rounding, and the golden adopts the same rule so software and
silicon agree bit for bit.

% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M0" Result — message width sweep (20 000 shots, seed 2024)
Table~\ref{tab:width} sweeps $(W,F)$ against the \texttt{f64} decoder on the
code-capacity model, 20\,000 shots per point. Every cell is within the
Monte-Carlo CI of \texttt{f64}, but $(8,3)$, Q5.3 with range $\pm15.9$ and
step $0.125$, tracks it most tightly and is the only 8-bit word that
reproduces the \texttt{f64} rate exactly at the $p=0.02$ floor
($1.0\times10^{-4}$).
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M0" Verdict (λ(0.02)≈3.9, λ(0.01)≈4.6)
It beats the finer $(8,4)$ there on dynamic range: at low $p$ the priors are
largest ($\lambda(0.02)\approx3.9$, $\lambda(0.01)\approx4.6$) and messages
accumulate, so the integer range matters more than a fractional bit. Below 8
bits, $(6,2)$ and $(7,3)$ bias high by $2$--$2.5\times$ at the floor, where
accuracy matters most: 8 bits is the floor, not a margin. The truncating blend
acts as mild extra damping, which is why Q5.3 sits slightly below \texttt{f64}
($0.95$--$0.97\times$) at higher $p$.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M0" Silicon sizing at (8,3)
At Q5.3 the message RAM ($432$ edges $\times2\times8$\,b) is 864\,B, about
$8\times$ smaller than at \texttt{f64}.

\subsection*{Leg/iteration schedule}

% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M5 (step 1)" Why (cycles = legs·iters·3+1; 4×25 = 301 cyc = 3.16 µs at 95.2 MHz)
In the unrolled RTL a worst-case decode takes
$3\cdot\mathrm{legs}\cdot\mathrm{iters}+1$ cycles, so the schedule is the
dominant latency term: the software default $4\times25$ costs 301 cycles,
\SI{3.16}{\micro\second} at the \SI{95.2}{\mega\hertz} of the first KV260
build.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M5 (step 1)" Result (80 000 shots)
We swept (legs, iters) at the Q5.3 word on the code-capacity model, 80\,000
shots per point, against the full $4\times25$ decoder (Fig.~\ref{fig:schedule}).
First, at a fixed sweep budget the split matters more than the total: the
60-sweep schedules $6\times10$, $5\times12$, $4\times15$ and $3\times20$ all
cost 181 cycles (\SI{1.90}{\micro\second}), but at the discriminating $p=0.05$
only $6\times10$ stays within CI of the baseline ($1.057\times$, against
$1.065\times$, $1.067\times$ and $1.073\times$). Relay-BP's strength is
leg-to-leg disorder diversity, and iterations within a leg have diminishing
returns once BP has settled, so cycles are best spent on many short legs.
Second, the schedule alone cannot reach sub-microsecond latency: the only
sub-\si{\micro\second} schedule, $2\times15$ (91 cycles,
\SI{0.96}{\micro\second}), costs $1.295\times$ LER and is outside CI at every
$p$.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M5 step 2" Changes and Result table
Adopting $6\times10$ (a six-leg $\gamma$ ROM, legs 0--3 keeping their seeds)
and narrowing the blend accumulator from 32 to 16 bits (bit-exact, since
$|\mathrm{blend}|\lesssim5600$) cut the worst case from 301 to 181 cycles; the
KV260 build closed at \SI{96.0}{\mega\hertz}, \SI{1.89}{\micro\second},
$1.68\times$ faster than $4\times25$ at $1.06\times$ LER; the gain is almost
entirely the cycle cut.

\subsection*{Circuit-level decoding}

% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M5-followup — circuit-level DEM" Software (rounds=6, 4×25, Q5.3)
The same fixed-point decoder handles gate-noise syndromes. On the depth-7
circuit-level DEM at $\mathrm{rounds}=6$ with the $4\times25$ schedule, plain
Q5.3 relay-BP tracks the float decoder and its LER falls with $p$ below a
per-cycle threshold of about 0.3\,\%: $4\times10^{-3}$ at $p=0.002$,
$2\times10^{-2}$ at $p=0.003$, $6.5\times10^{-2}$ at $p=0.004$.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q5-circuit-dem.md "Results" per-cycle table and "Honest positioning"
This agrees with the software study, where the per-cycle rate of the $d=12$
code crossed below the $d=6$ member at $p\approx0.0025$--$0.003$; Bravyi et
al.\ report about 0.7\,\% for the same code, and since the DEM is Stim-exact
the remaining $2$--$2.5\times$ is decoder strength, not the model.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M5-followup — circuit-level DEM" RTL co-sim
The circuit-level graph is irregular: at $\mathrm{rounds}=1$ it has 864
variables, 144 checks and 2952 edges with maximum check degree 25, against a
uniform 6 at code capacity. The parametric sequential RTL decoder
(\S\ref{sec:architecture}) decodes 40 sampled DEM shots over this graph
bit-identically to the golden with no RTL change, only a regenerated Tanner
header.

\subsection*{No OSD tail (negative result)}

Relay-BP occasionally leaves a hard decision with $H\hat e\ne s$. We built the
classic fix, an OSD tail on the fixed-point posterior LLRs
(\texttt{FixedRelayBpOsd}), as a slow path on the processor side gated by the
RTL's \texttt{valid\_flag}, and measured whether it earns its place.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M5-followup — the OSD-0 tail" Result, code capacity (20 000 shots)
At code capacity (20\,000 shots) OSD-0 is LER-neutral at every $p$ within CI
while the tail rate grows from 0.15\,\% to 8.6\,\% over $p=0.03\to0.06$: the
residual failures are mostly uncorrectable (weight above $d/2$), so a valid
OSD decode is a near coin-flip between cosets.
% src: docs/perf/qec-q7-fixed-bp.md "M5-followup — the OSD-0 tail" Result, circuit-level table (rounds=6, 3000 shots)
At circuit level ($\mathrm{rounds}=6$, 3000 shots) OSD-0 is worse than no tail
in both fixed and float arithmetic: at $p=0.002$ the plain fixed/float LER
$4.0\times10^{-3}$/$3.3\times10^{-3}$ rises to
$6.7\times10^{-3}$/$5.7\times10^{-3}$, and at $p=0.003$
$2.0\times10^{-2}$/$1.9\times10^{-2}$ rises to
$2.7\times10^{-2}$/$2.8\times10^{-2}$; replacing an invalid guess by a valid
but often wrong-coset decode loses more shots than it rescues. Order 4 is
about break-even, and the beneficial regime is order 12
($1.7\times10^{-3}$/$1.3\times10^{-3}$ and
$1.3\times10^{-2}$/$1.5\times10^{-2}$), i.e.\ $2^{12}=4096$
reliability-ordered GF(2) re-solves per failed shot: exactly the
data-dependent, variable-latency work the hardware track set out to avoid.
Fixed Q5.3 tracks float at every order, so the word is not the limiter; the
order is. The decoder therefore ships as pure relay-BP with no OSD tail.
